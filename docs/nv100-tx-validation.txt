NV100 bounded TX/RX smoke check — 2026-10-21

Result
Four finite same-card tone observations passed: CS16 and CF32 TX, each at
approximately +200 kHz and +400 kHz relative to a 915 MHz center frequency.
The strongest received spectral peak followed both requested offsets, rose
34.94–39.01 dB above a TX-off capture in the expected frequency region, and
was 37.06–38.98 dB above the capture's median spectral power. Peak sample
magnitude stayed below 1% of CS16 full scale; no clipping was observed.
Detailed measurements are in docs/nv100-tx-results.csv.

The selected logical channels were TX 0 (A1) and RX 2 (B1). Live SDK-backed
antenna readback reported TX skiq_rf_port_J1 and RX skiq_rf_port_J2. The card
had two antennas attached; their antenna bands and coupling were unmeasured.
The test used 5 MS/s and 4 MHz bandwidth, manual RX gain 34 dB, maximum
SDK-advertised TX attenuation 41.75 dB, and waveform amplitude 0.02 of host
full scale. Named attenuation readback confirmed the requested 41.75 dB.
No external attenuator, calibrated receiver, or power meter was used.
Absolute RF output power was not measured or inferred from these controls.

The public NV100 hardware manual supports A1 TX and B1 RX on different RF
ports. Its nominal TX gain-control range is 48 dB, but the live SDK reports
41.75 dB maximum for this card. An initial harness guard expecting at least
48 dB stopped before any TX stream was activated. The corrected run used
actual SDK bounds, readback, and further-reduced waveform amplitude. This
was a harness assumption, not an observed driver failure.
Reference: Epiq Sidekiq NV100 Hardware User Manual v1.4, pages 14, 17 and 20:
https://resources.epiqsolutions.com/hubfs/Epiq-Solutions-Sidekiq-Nv100-Hardware-User-Manual.pdf?hsLang=en

Method and scope
The harness checked logical handle and physical-port mappings, configured
profiles before tuning, disabled the internal counter data source, verified
maximum named attenuation, and captured a TX-off baseline for each format.
It then submitted whole 16,380-sample MTUs with a bounded 0.20-second loop
for each tone. Immediate TX deactivation followed the loop; accepted sample
counts describe host submission, not a measurement of radiated duration or
samples. Each on-capture contains 262,144 complex CS16 RX samples; each
baseline contains 131,072. A full-period tone buffer avoids restart artifacts.
Actual requested offsets were 199938.949939 and 399877.899878 Hz.

Three later capture windows were analyzed with 16,384-point Hann FFTs. Their
median power spectrum supplied the strongest peak, expected-band power and
median noise reference. Frequency tolerance was two FFT bins, 610.35 Hz.
Pass criteria required the strongest peak at the expected tone, at least
10 dB over median power, at least 6 dB above TX-off expected-band power, and
less than 80% full-scale peak magnitude. Synthetic detector checks accepted
the expected tone and rejected a different tone and noise-only input.

Each +400 kHz capture reported one RX overflow after analysis paused the
consumer between tone runs; both recovered and supplied the complete requested
capture. No counter continuity or gap-free streaming claim is made for this
FFT check. The separate digital RX validation covers gap reporting and aligned
recovery with the internal counter source.

Both formats transmitted host data and produced the expected same-card RF
observations. This does not distinguish antenna coupling from internal leakage,
measure antenna efficiency or port isolation, establish RF output power or
spectral compliance, or validate dual-channel, timed, non-MTU, or long-running
TX. It is a short smoke check of the existing immediate single-channel TX/RX
path and named attenuation control on one NV100.

Cleanup and evidence
TX and RX streams were stopped and closed, original attenuation, RX gain,
gain mode and counter settings were restored, and the card reported available.
Rate, bandwidth and tuning remain at the test values. The installed SDK symlink
still selects 4.25.0; /usr/lib/epiq and loaded kernel modules were unchanged.
The 4.26 SDK and tested driver used private staging and installation prefixes.
Raw captures, harness, output and the corrected/initial results are retained
privately in the audit workspace; no raw RF captures are added to the repo.
